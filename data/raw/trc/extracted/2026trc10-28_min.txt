2. COMMUNICATIONS TO THE COMMISSION AND PUBLIC COMMENT
Chair White asked if there was any communication to the Tax Review Commission (TRC).
Dr. Karacaovali said there were two written testimonies received and circulated to the
commissioners prior to the meeting (and will be posted on the TRC webpage). He also invited
public to come forward using the chat/Q&A function over Zoom. Devin Thomas (Director of
Tax & Budget Policy from Hawai‘i Appleseed Center for Law & Economic Justice), who was
representing the Hawai‘i Tax Fairness Coalition, had a public comment to provide.
Mr. Thomas highlighted the following policy priorities:
a) Continued review of the conveyance tax, including consideration of a marginal rate system
and possible controlled interest provision to ensure high‑value, non–owner‑occupied properties
face appropriate taxation.
b) Use of conveyance tax or capital gains revenue to support tax credits for working families,
emphasizing affordability and retention of local residents.
c) Encouragement for the Commission to examine tax credits more broadly as instruments to
support household financial stability.
No additional public comments were offered.

4. PRESENTATION BY TRC CHAIR JONATHAN WHITE ON “SINGLE SALES
FACTOR” AND TRC DISCUSSION
Chair White presented a detailed written analysis (previously posted on the Commission website)
proposing that Hawai‘i transition from its current equally-weighted three-factor corporate
income tax apportionment formula (property, payroll, sales) to a single sales factor (SSF)
formula.
Key Themes Presented:
1. Policy Alignment & Competitiveness
Most states have already adopted SSF; Hawaii is increasingly an outlier.

Commission Discussion Highlights:

• Commissioners emphasized the need for modeling, noting that effects may vary by

5. PRESENTATION BY TRC CHAIR JONATHAN WHITE ON “CONVEYANCE TAX
– CONTROLLING INTEREST PROVISION” AND TRC DISCUSSION
Chair White presented a conceptual outline of a recommendation to address longstanding
horizontal inequity between taxpayers who transfer real property directly (and pay conveyance
tax) and those who transfer ownership indirectly through sale of a controlling interest in an entity
holding real property (currently not taxed).
Key Themes Presented:
1. Policy Rationale
Approximately 17 states impose conveyance tax on transfers of controlling
o
interests.
Hawaii has considered legislation repeatedly (2013, 2014, 2016, 2024, 2026) but
o
none has passed.
2. Design Approaches
Broad application: apply the tax to all entities holding real property.
o
Narrow application: limit to entities primarily engaged in real estate business or
o
exceeding asset thresholds.
Valuation methods debated in past bills include fair market value, county assessed
o
value, or the lesser of assessed value and a qualified appraisal.
3. Administrative Considerations
Some testimony in past sessions emphasized complexity; Chair White noted that
o
other states administer such provisions successfully.
Commission Discussion Highlights:
• Commissioner Ueoka described practical challenges with existing conveyance tax rules
for LLC transfers, exemptions, and uncertainty in administrative guidance.
• Commissioners discussed potential impacts on small businesses, real estate transactions,
and compliance burdens.
• Commissioner Fukunaga raised concerns about structuring and avoidance issues in large
commercial real estate transactions, highlighting the need for careful definitions of
“change of control.”
• Commissioners agreed the inequity is significant, but further research is required to
determine administrative feasibility and appropriate design details.
Chair White will evaluate whether to prepare a fuller recommendation.

6. COMPETITIVENESS FRAMEWORK DISCUSSION
The Commission continued its strategic discussion on how the forthcoming competitiveness
section of the final report should be structured.
Key Themes Raised:
1. Defining Competitiveness
Commissioner Fukunaga emphasized Hawaii’s stagnant real per-capita income
o
over two decades and the relationship between economic opportunity,
out-migration, and tax structure.
Competitiveness should focus on attracting talent, capital, and business formation,
o
rather than simply adjusting tax rates.
2. Potential Ingredients for a "Competitiveness Cocktail"
Improving tax neutrality by shifting burdens away from mobile factors (labor,
o
capital) and toward more stable bases.
Considering measures to encourage remote-friendly mainland companies to hire
o
Hawaii residents.
Enhancing Department of Taxation capacity, especially collections, which could
o
reduce the tax gap and improve fairness.
Evaluating adjustments to the wholesale GET rate or rules to reduce pyramiding
o
and improve business cost structures.
3. Provocative / High-Level Ideas (Not Endorsed)
Chair White noted theoretical large-scale reforms (e.g., VAT replacing income
o
tax), but acknowledged such concepts exceed the Commission’s practical scope.
4. Upcoming Input
The Commission will receive a future presentation from Dr. Nicholas Lacoste
o
(co-researcher with Dr. Dylan Moore at University of Hawai‘i, and co-
investigator for the UHERO study fir TRC) regarding the Transient
Accommodations Tax (TAT).
