Objective:
Modernize Hawai‘i’s corporate income tax to better address the modern global economy and
ensure its robustness in the face of federal changes.
Proposed Recommendation:
Further study and consider options for modernizing and bolstering Hawai‘i’s corporate
income tax, including a transition to mandatory worldwide combined reporting as well as the
alternatives described below.
Narrative:
The legislature is aware of this topic and has requested the Department to study the issue
further and develop a revenue estimate for it.1 The Commission believes its research and
description of the issues will add to the Department’s eventual report and to the general
discourse. This narrative does not represent a complete or comprehensive
recommendation. Rather, this is a recommendation to further study the topic.
As described above (note: this refers to the report’s single sales factor writeup), state
corporate income taxes, including Hawai‘i’s, use apportionment formulas to apportion (split
up) taxpayers’ taxable income among the states. This calculation involves two things, the
first is the apportionment formula itself and the second is the taxable income that is split up
by the apportionment formula. The Commission’s earlier recommendation, to transition to
single sales factor apportionment, concerned the actual apportionment formula. This
recommendation, on the other hand, considers the universe of taxable income that is
exposed to the apportionment formula.
To determine this pot of income, Hawai‘i’s corporate income tax utilizes water’s edge
combined reporting.2 This means that only the income of domestic entities is included and
exposed to Hawai‘i’s apportionment factor to determine Hawai‘i’s share. Transitioning to a

Mandatory worldwide combined reporting is constitutional. However, it is not fully used by
any states currently.3 The cases holding mandatory worldwide combined reporting
constitutional are Container Corporation,4 and Barclays Bank.5 At the time Container was
decided, more than 10 states required worldwide combined reporting.6 Despite this, there
was much consternation about mandatory worldwide combined reporting at the time, and a
grand bargain was reached where the states agreed to back away from worldwide combined
reporting and the federal government agreed to help the states enforce their system.7
An important part of the federal government’s role was enforcement of transfer pricing.8
Transfer pricing is important to the federal system, but less important to the states’ systems
due to the widespread use of combined reporting. Insert description of transfer pricing and
the reason the combined reporting method obviates its need.
However, transfer pricing is still important to the states. This is because if states’ combined
reporting systems are limited to the water’s edge, as they are, foreign affiliates will be
excluded from the combined report and the advantages of combined reporting will
disappear when multinational entities are involved. Thus, even with combined reporting, the
only thing preventing multinational entities from taking full advantage of this and transferring
income to excluded foreign affiliates, at will, is transfer pricing. The federal government’s
enforcement of its transfer pricing regime was important to the efficacy of the state system.9

Model Law:
There is a model statute available for mandatory worldwide combined reporting.14 The
model is based on the Multistate Tax Commission’s combined reporting model, which
Hawai‘i’s corporate income tax is at least loosely based on.15 The Commission recommends
the model statute as a good starting point.
Options:
Adopting mandatory worldwide combined reporting will be a heavy lift, especially politically.
It is a step no state has taken since the 1980s. Thus, the Commission provides the following
options that may be less onerous but still serve to modernize the Hawai‘i’s corporate income
tax.
Elective worldwide combined reporting:
Several states require worldwide combined reporting but allow taxpayer’s to elect into
water’s edge combined reporting, these include California,16 North Dakota,17 Montana,18

One common issue is the duration of the election. Colorado requires a 10-year election,
meaning that once a taxpayer elects into water’s edge, that taxpayer is bound to that election
for 10 tax years.23
A simple and easy to understand issue with this option is that taxpayers will only elect into
worldwide combined reporting if doing so would save them money. Thus, the revenue
estimate for this option would be negative. The Commission still considers this a viable
option both as a modernization of the corporate income tax and as a potential stepping
stone toward mandatory worldwide combined reporting.
Net CFC-tested Income:
Net CFC-tested income, or, NCTI, is another option. The source of NCTI is Internal Revenue
Code section 951A. States can conform to NCTI as a substitute to mandating worldwide
combined reporting. Taxpayers are required to report the amount of NCTI on their federal
returns, so it is a readily available figure that will not require additional filing or disclosure
from taxpayers. However, the attributes of the CFCs generating the NCTI would ideally be
included in the taxpayer’s apportionment factor. This could be complicated, but no more
complicated that the same effort under mandatory worldwide combined reporting. In 2026,
New Mexico conformed to IRC section 951A along with full factor representation. If this
option is chosen, the Commission recommends their bill and law as a guide.24
