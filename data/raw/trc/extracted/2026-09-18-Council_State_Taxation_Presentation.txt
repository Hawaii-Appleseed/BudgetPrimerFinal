COST’s Policy Positions
COST is a non-profit trade association of over 450 multi-
jurisdictional companies.
COST's advocacy is governed by a broad set of public policy
objectives. When advocating on issues, COST first looks to its
official policy statements, which are approved by the COST Board of
Directors.
COST does not take a position on a state’s overall level of taxation
or on tax rates. COST’s objective is to preserve and promote
equitable and nondiscriminatory state and local taxation of
multijurisdictional business entities.
COST’s Policy Statements are publicly available at: www.cost.org. 4

===== page 11 =====
Business Perspective on Hawaii’s GET
Good stuff:
• Low GET rate: 4.5% rate (4.0 state plus .5% in most counties) vs. U.S. average of 7.5% state and local sales tax rate
• Broad tax base inclusion of B2C goods and services
Deviation from Optimal Consumption Tax Design
• Widespread taxation of B2B (business inputs) resulting in tax pyramiding
• No full sale for resale exemption: .5% rate for wholesaling and manufacturing activities, but full rate for sales of
most business inputs
• Utilization of outdated “turnover” tax model
Regressivity impact of high GET and excise share of all Hawaii state and local taxes
• Offset by graduated rate personal income and corporate income taxes
• Hawaii is in the middle of the states in ITEP’s progressivity/regressivity study
• Tax systems balance progressivity (ability to pay) with revenue balance and stability
Hawaii already taxes digital advertising as part of its GET base and therefore does not
need to enact a separate gross receipts tax on digital advertising

Best Practices for Hawaii
Things Hawaii can do to improve tax administration:
• Extend the due date for filing corporate income tax returns one month or more
beyond the federal extended due date
• Expand the 60 day safe harbor for nonresident traveling employees to reporting,
not just withholding
• Enhance Federal audit change reporting
• Provide adequate time for appeals (90 days)
• Apply even-handed interest rates

===== page 26 =====
Hawaii Corporate Income Tax Overview
• The Hawaii CIT rate of 6.4% on all taxable income over $100,000 is close to the
national average state/local CiT rate.
• Hawaii requires Water’s Edge Combined Reporting as do a majority of other states.
• Hawaii does not require mandatory Worldwide Combined Reporting – consistent
with all other states.
• Hawaii does not include GILTI o r NCTI (foreign source income) in its CIT base –
consistent with a slight majority of all states.
• Hawaii’s conformity to key CIT provisions of OBBBA – static with conformity for
168(j) only (not 174 and rest of 168)
• Hawaii applies equally weighted three factor apportionment for corp income tax
unlike the majority of states that use single sales factor
