4. PRESENTATION BY TRC CHAIR JONATHAN WHITE ON “WORLDWIDE
COMBINED REPORTING (WCR) AND NET CONTROLLED FOREIGN
CORPORATION TESTED INCOME (NCTI)” AND TRC DISCUSSION
Chair Jonathan White provided a formal introduction of a potential recommendation concerning
worldwide combined reporting and the taxation of Net Controlled Foreign Corporation Tested
Income (NCTI). He explained that the topic is being considered as a potential recommendation
for further study in the Commission’s final report rather than as a fully developed legislative
proposal.
The Chair summarized the difference between Hawaii’s current water’s-edge approach and a
worldwide combined reporting system. Under worldwide combined reporting, income and
apportionment factors of foreign affiliates would be included in the calculation of taxable income
apportioned to Hawai‘i. He discussed historical developments involving transfer pricing
enforcement, federal tax policy changes, and the evolution of international taxation, including
GILTI and NCTI provisions.
The Chair noted that several states either permit or require forms of worldwide combined
reporting and referenced recent legislative developments in other jurisdictions. He emphasized
the structural and policy rationale for examining the issue rather than focusing solely on potential
revenue effects.

5. PRESENTATION BY TRC CHAIR JONATHAN WHITE ON “COMBINED
VOLUNTARY DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT (VDA) AND ADMINISTRATIVE
RECOMMENDATION” AND TRC DISCUSSION
Chair White introduced a discussion regarding Hawaii’s voluntary disclosure agreement (VDA)
program and potential measures to improve tax compliance and collections. The discussion
originated from previous suggestions concerning voluntary disclosure procedures and methods to
reduce future delinquent accounts.
The Chair outlined several concepts for consideration, including:
• Creating a more attractive VDA program to encourage earlier taxpayer participation.
• Considering shorter look-back periods.
• Allowing non-anonymous participation and registration at the time of application.
• Maintaining existing VDA programs as alternative pathways.
• Providing additional support for departmental collection activities.
Commissioner Jon Yasuda suggested developing more standardized installment payment
arrangements similar to the federal system, allowing taxpayers with relatively modest balances to
enter repayment plans through a streamlined process. He noted that greater simplicity and
predictability could encourage taxpayer compliance.
Commissioners discussed broader support for the Department of Taxation (DOTAX), including
workforce development, training, organizational resources, and operational improvements.
Commissioner Mark Fukunaga raised the possibility of utilizing artificial intelligence to assist
with tax administration, compliance reviews, and discrepancy detection.
Dr. Karacaovali reported that the Department has been examining artificial intelligence
applications and has thus far focused on administrative uses and experimentation with synthetic
data because of taxpayer confidentiality concerns. He noted ongoing efforts to evaluate future
implementation options while preserving data security requirements.

6. PRESENTATION BY DR. NICHOLAS LACOSTE (CO-INVESTIGATOR OF THE
UNIVERSITY OF HAWAI‘I ECONOMIC RESEARCH ORGANIZATION [UHERO]
TRC PROJECT) ON THE IMPACT OF TRANSIENT ACCOMMODATIONS TAX
AND TRC DISCUSSION
The Commission received a presentation from Dr. Nicholas Lacoste, who discussed ongoing
research regarding the Transient Accommodations Tax (TAT), its revenue-generating efficiency,
and its broader effects on Hawaii’s tax system. Dr. Dylan Moore also participated in portions of
the discussion.
Presentation Summary
Dr. Lacoste explained that the TAT is Hawaii’s third-largest source of tax revenue and reviewed
research intended to estimate how changes in TAT rates affect tax collections and visitor
behavior. Using economic modeling and a synthetic-control methodology that compares Hawai‘i
with tourism-intensive jurisdictions elsewhere in the United States and Guam, the study seeks to
estimate the revenue effects of TAT increases.
Key points from the presentation included:
• Historical TAT increases generally resulted in higher TAT revenue.
• Tourist behavioral responses appear to reduce, but not eliminate, the revenue gains from
TAT increases.
• The analysis suggests Hawai‘i likely remains below the revenue-maximizing TAT rate
when examining TAT collections alone.
• When broader impacts on General Excise Tax (GET) collections and economic activity
are considered, revenue effects become less certain.
• TAT increases may affect visitor spending patterns and indirectly influence sectors such
as recreation, attractions, dining, and wages.
Dr. Lacoste emphasized that policymakers should evaluate TAT changes within the context of
the entire tax system rather than viewing the tax in isolation.
Commissioner Discussion
Commissioners engaged in an extensive discussion regarding the methodology, assumptions, and
policy implications of the analysis.
Commissioner Mark Fukunaga asked questions concerning the relationship between TAT
changes and GET collections, elasticity estimates, exchange-rate influences, and differences in

7. COMPETITIVENESS AND TAX POLICY DISCUSSION
Upon reconvening, the Commission resumed discussion of tax competitiveness and possible
recommendations for inclusion in the Commission's final report. The Chair described the effort
as developing a “competitiveness” framework consisting of multiple complementary policy
recommendations.
Commissioner Mark Fukunaga highlighted several recommendations that had been advanced by
prior Tax Review Commissions, including:
• Indexing tax brackets or rates to inflation.
• Examining taxation of pension income.
• Expanding business investment incentives through accelerated depreciation or related
provisions.

Commissioners discussed the relationship between consumption taxes, income taxes, corporate
taxes, and incentives for economic growth.
Topics included:
• Potential use of General Excise Tax adjustments.
• Earned Income Tax Credit expansion as a means of offsetting regressive impacts.
• Corporate income tax competitiveness.
• Accelerated depreciation provisions.
• Single-sales-factor apportionment.
• Tax exporting to nonresidents.
Dr. Karacaovali noted that previous Tax Review Commissions had generally opposed increased
reliance on consumption taxes because of concerns regarding regressivity. He also provided
updated estimates indicating that adoption of single-sales-factor apportionment could generate
approximately $35 million in additional corporate income tax revenue under current economic
conditions.
Dr. Moore discussed the advantages of pairing single-sales-factor apportionment with
accelerated depreciation provisions and described those policies as potentially improving
investment incentives while reducing distortions associated with business location decisions. He
also emphasized that substantial uncertainty remains regarding the optimal level of corporate
taxation.
No formal action was taken. Commissioners agreed that additional discussion would occur
during future meetings as the report is finalized.
