4. DISCUSSION OF §1031 CLAWBACK PROPOSAL
Commissioner Jessica Enos presented a proposal to adopt a "§1031 clawback" provision for
Hawai‘i. She explained that, under current law, gains deferred through a like-kind exchange may
ultimately escape Hawai‘i taxation when replacement property is located in another state and
later sold in a taxable transaction. The proposal would allow Hawai‘i to recapture and tax
deferred gains attributable to Hawai‘i property when the replacement property is ultimately
disposed of.
The discussion focused on:
• Examples of similar laws adopted in California, Montana, and Oregon.
• Annual reporting requirements used by other states to track deferred gains.
• Administrative considerations, including electronic filing and annual compliance
reminders.
• Potential revenue protection benefits for Hawai‘i.
• Interaction with resident and nonresident taxation rules in other states.
• Possible issues involving credits for taxes paid to other jurisdictions.
Members generally expressed interest in the proposal. Commissioner Mark Fukunaga asked
about the potential magnitude of deferred gains and administrative costs. Chair Jonathan White
noted that additional analysis may be needed regarding interstate credit issues and
implementation details.
No vote was taken. Commissioner Enos agreed to continue developing the written
recommendation for future consideration.

5. DISCUSSION OF MARGINAL RATE STRUCTURE FOR CONVEYANCE TAX
Commissioner Jeffrey Ueoka presented a proposal to convert the conveyance tax from its current
bracket structure to a marginal tax rate structure. The proposal builds upon concepts previously
considered by the Legislature and is intended to reduce cliff effects created when transactions
cross threshold amounts.
Members discussed:
• Improving horizontal equity by avoiding abrupt tax increases at bracket breakpoints.
• Potential impacts on homeowners and non-owner-occupied transactions.
• Possible effects on housing supply and market behavior.
• The importance of reviewing revenue implications before finalizing a recommendation.
Dr. Baybars Karacaovali indicated that revenue estimates could be developed to evaluate
whether modifications would be needed to maintain revenue neutrality. Members generally
expressed support for the concept, and Commissioner Ueoka agreed to continue refining the
proposal.
No vote was taken.
6. SINGLE SALES FACTOR APPORTIONMENT
The Commission resumed its ongoing discussion regarding adoption of a single sales factor
apportionment formula for corporate income tax purposes. Chair Jonathan White summarized
prior discussions and noted that alternative approaches, such as double-weighted sales factors
and phased transitions, could still be discussed in the report as implementation options.
Members expressed support for recommending a single sales factor while identifying alternative
transition approaches for legislative consideration.
Motion
Commissioner Jessica Enos moved that the Commission adopt a recommendation supporting a
single sales factor apportionment formula as part of the competitiveness discussion and as a
standalone recommendation.
Roll Call Vote

• Commissioner Jessica Enos – Aye
• Commissioner Mark Fukunaga – Aye
• Commissioner Stacey Katakura – Aye

8. WORLDWIDE COMBINED REPORTING (WCR) AND NCTI
The Commission continued discussing the proposal concerning Worldwide Combined Reporting
and Net Controlled Foreign Corporation Tested Income (NCTI). The Chair characterized the
topic as one appropriate for further study rather than an immediate legislative recommendation
and noted that the Legislature had already expressed interest in additional examination of the
subject.
Members agreed that additional discussion would be appropriate before a vote is considered.
9. COMBINED VOLUNTARY DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT AND

• Administrative concerns previously discussed regarding tax credit certification deadlines.
• Suggestions for strengthening the Offer in Compromise process.

10. TRC IDENTIFICATION AND DISCUSSION OF THE FOUNDATIONS FOR THE
COMPETITIVENESS TOPIC
Following the recess, the Commission continued its broader discussion regarding state tax
competitiveness, economic growth, and housing affordability.
Commissioner Mark Fukunaga presented observations regarding Hawai‘i's long-term economic
performance, including slower growth relative to national trends, persistent out-migration, and
housing shortages. He suggested that increasing housing supply could serve as a significant
economic development strategy and discussed tax policies that might encourage investment in
residential construction and infrastructure.
Members discussed:
• Accelerated depreciation as an incentive for housing development.
• Housing affordability and supply.
• Economic development incentives and tax credit design.
• The relationship between competitiveness and tax policy.
• Potential future consideration of wealth tax and estate tax issues.
• Various conceptual tax policy "packages" involving housing, business competitiveness,
and tax-exporting strategies.
Commissioners Mark Fukunaga and Stacey Katakura agreed to continue developing materials
regarding housing-related competitiveness recommendations.
No formal action was taken.
