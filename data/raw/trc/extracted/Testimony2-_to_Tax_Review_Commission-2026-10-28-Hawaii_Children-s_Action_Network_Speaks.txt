To: Hawai‘i State Tax Review Commission (TRC)
Re: TRC identification and discussion of three foundations for the competitiveness topic (agenda
item #6)
Time: October 28, 2026, 2:00 PM
Dear Chair White, Vice Chair Chock, and Members of the Commission,
On behalf of Hawaiʻi Children’s Action Network Speaks! (HCAN Speaks!) , I am writing to urge the TRC to
broaden your understanding of competitiveness to reflect two fundamental questions:
1. How does Hawaiʻi compete with other states to ensure that our families with children can afford
to stay here?
2. How does our keiki’s well-being compare with that of children and youth in other states?
A competitive state is not simply one that attracts businesses. It is one where families can afford
housing, food, child care, transportation, and health care; parents can remain in the workforce; and
children have the resources and opportunities they need to grow up healthy, succeed in school, and
build their futures here.
Hawaiʻi’s high cost of living places enormous pressure on families. When parents struggle to afford basic
necessities, children face greater risks of housing instability, food insecurity, and unmet health needs.
These challenges affect children’s educational outcomes and long-term well-being. They also affect
employers, who depend on a stable workforce and families who can afford to remain in Hawaiʻi.
HCAN Speaks! believes that children’s well-being is an essential measure of Hawaiʻi’s economic
competitiveness—not a separate consideration. Therefore, we urge you to evaluate tax policies based
on how they affect families with children, particularly those with limited incomes, and whether they
help create the conditions our keiki need to thrive and for their families to stay here.
We also urge you to fully address the Legislature’s requests to the TRC contained in SR19 SD1 (2025),
and HCR182 (2025).
For example, HCR182 calls for consideration of tax equity, the distribution of the tax burden among
residents and nonresidents, the effectiveness of tax credits and exemptions, and the adequacy and
stability of public revenue. These issues directly affect families and children. Tax credits can help families
afford necessities, while reliable public revenue supports education, health care, and other services
essential to children’s development. We hope the TRC will explicitly address these goals and evaluate
who benefits from tax policies, who bears their costs, and what public benefits they deliver.

Term Care Financing Program, including financing mechanisms, implementation costs, and possible

In summary, HCAN Speaks! respectfully urges the TRC to:
1. Redefine competitiveness to include family affordability, child well-being, educational
outcomes, health, and families’ ability to remain in Hawaiʻi.
2. Address the equity and revenue goals in HCR182, including the distribution of tax burdens, the
effectiveness of tax preferences, and sustainable funding for essential public services.
3. Respond substantively to SR19 SD1 by evaluating financing options and implementation costs
for the Long-Term Care Financing Program.
4. Measure tax policy by its real-world effects on families with children, including whether it
reduces economic hardship and supports the conditions children need to thrive.
Hawaiʻi’s future depends on whether families can afford to raise their children here and whether our
keiki have the opportunity to succeed. Tax policy should be evaluated not only by its effect on
businesses, but also by whether it helps make Hawaiʻi a place where children and families can thrive.
