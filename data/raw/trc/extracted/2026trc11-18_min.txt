6. TRC IDENTIFICATION AND DISCUSSION OF THE FOUNDATIONS FOR THE
COMPETITIVENESS TOPIC
The Commission continued its broad discussion regarding tax policy changes that could improve
Hawai'i’s competitiveness, affordability, and economic opportunities.
Key concepts discussed included:
Housing Affordability and Homeownership
Commissioner Jessica Enos highlighted housing affordability as a principal concern contributing
to outmigration. She suggested evaluating tax policies that could assist residents in purchasing
homes and retaining younger residents in Hawai'i.
Ideas discussed included:
• Expanding incentives for homeownership;
• First-time homebuyer assistance mechanisms;
• Mortgage-related tax credits;
• Measures to encourage greater housing availability.
Dr. Baybars Karacaovali noted that recent legislation had already expanded the Individual
Housing Account program through Act 217, Session Laws of Hawai'i 2026, substantially
increasing contribution and deduction limits beginning in future tax years.
Demand-Side Versus Supply-Side Housing Policies
Members engaged in an extended discussion with UHERO economist Dr. Dylan Moore
regarding housing economics. Dr. Moore explained that demand-side assistance programs may
have limited effectiveness when housing supply remains constrained and may primarily increase
housing prices rather than overall affordability. He emphasized that increasing housing supply
generally offers a more durable solution to affordability concerns.
Commissioners discussed whether homebuyer-focused incentives may nevertheless be useful if
they help first-time buyers gain access to available housing units.
Accelerated Depreciation for Housing Development
Commissioner Mark Fukunaga suggested that accelerated or bonus depreciation could be
targeted specifically toward housing development, particularly rental housing. Members
discussed whether such an incentive could improve project economics and help stimulate
housing construction. Several commissioners viewed a housing-focused depreciation incentive
more favorably than a general business expensing provision because it directly addresses the
state's housing shortage.

Vacancy Tax and Housing Supply
The Commission discussed vacancy taxes and similar measures designed to encourage
residential occupancy and increase housing availability. Members noted that such proposals may
involve real property tax issues that fall largely within county authority. Nevertheless, some
members expressed interest in discussing such concepts within the Commission's narrative or
policy recommendations.
The discussion also referenced research suggesting that increasing housing supply at higher price
points may indirectly improve affordability throughout the housing market by freeing housing
stock through a chain of transactions.
Mortgage Credit Concepts
Commissioner Jon Yasuda suggested examining a Hawai'i counterpart to the federal Mortgage
Credit Certificate program, which provides benefits to first-time homebuyers. Members
discussed whether a state-level version could assist younger residents seeking to enter the
housing market.
7. IDENTIFICATION OF OTHER TOPICS FOR TRC MEMBER PRESENTATIONS
AT FUTURE MEETINGS
Section 1031 Exchange "Clawback" Proposal
Commissioner Enos introduced an idea modeled after laws in other states that would preserve
Hawai'i’s ability to tax gains attributable to Hawai'i property when property owners complete
Section 1031 exchanges into property located outside the state. Commissioner Enos agreed to
prepare and circulate supporting materials before a future meeting. The Chair directed that the
topic be placed on a future agenda.
Remote Work and Workforce Retention
Commissioner Enos also raised the possibility of tax incentives aimed at encouraging mainland
employers to hire Hawai'i-based remote workers. Members discussed administrative and legal
considerations, including potential constitutional and interstate commerce concerns. No
consensus was reached.
Renewable Energy Tax Credit Administration
Commissioner Enos raised concerns regarding recently enacted certification requirements for
residential renewable energy tax credits, particularly the requirement that taxpayers certify
adjusted gross income before final income information may be available. Members discussed
whether the requirement could create administrative difficulties and uncertainty for taxpayers.
The Chair indicated that the issue might be considered within a broader tax administration
recommendation rather than as a standalone recommendation.
