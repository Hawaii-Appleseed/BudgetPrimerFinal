4. TRC DISCUSSION OF THE UNIVERSITY OF HAWAI‘I ECONOMIC RESEARCH
ORGANIZATION (UHERO) DRAFT REPORT AND DEVELOPMENT OF
FEEDBACK TO UHERO
4.1 Summary of Primary Meeting Discussion
Chair White summarized the prior meeting’s discussion of the UHERO draft report, noting:
• Commissioners had focused primarily on GET revenue from visitors, tax pyramiding,
and potential options to mitigate pyramiding, such as adjustments to the wholesale rate or
broader structural changes to the GET.
• Competitiveness emerged as an overarching theme, particularly how tax structure affects
business activity and resident/visitor cost burdens.
• An update was provided the prior meeting by Commissioner Mark regarding a discussion
with the Governor, focused largely on estate tax policy and potential mechanisms to
encourage high-net-worth individuals to remain and invest in Hawaiʻi through
donation‑linked incentives. Although Commissioners expressed caution, Chair White
indicated such concepts may appear only as “items for further study”, rather than formal
recommendations.
4.2 Commissioner Reactions and Report‑Review Status
• Several commissioners noted they were still working through the 170+ page report.
• Commissioners expressed appreciation for the breadth of data but noted structure,
navigability, and executive summaries may be helpful.
• Consultant Dr. Moore welcomed suggestions for improving presentation in the final
report.

A substantial portion of the meeting focused on developing a framework for the Commission's
upcoming final report, including identifying top themes, potential policy recommendations, and
structuring the report’s “competitiveness” section.
5.1 Request to Narrow Priorities
Vice-Chair Chock prompted discussion by suggesting the Commission select three major topics
or priority areas, given limited time. Chair White responded that the Commission may ultimately
produce 5–7 total recommendations, some grouped under competitiveness and others as
standalone topics.
5.2 Possible Recommendation Areas Discussed
Themes raised throughout the meeting included:
a. Competitiveness Improvements
• Single sales factor apportionment (Commissioners White & Enos)
• Adjustments to income tax rates, especially at high-income brackets
• Potential 30-day safe harbor for remote workers or business travelers to reduce
withholding burdens
• Revising TAT administration, including possible recentralization at the State level
• General exploration of strategies to reduce Hawaii’s cost of doing business
b. Improvements to GET Structure
• Addressing tax pyramiding
• Clarifying the effective GET tax base, especially given Hawaii’s tourism composition
• Considering exemptions or reconfigurations that improve fairness while mitigating
revenue loss
c. Inflation Indexing
Commissioners discussed prior TRC recommendations to index tax brackets and standard
deduction amounts to inflation.
• Political and administrative barriers to past implementation were noted.
• Consultant Dr. Moore explained that non-indexing is effectively a stealth tax increase
over time.
• Commissioners questioned whether a phased or delayed-effective-date indexing proposal
might be more politically viable.
d. Estate Tax Policies
• Ideas discussed during the Governor’s meeting—such as donation-based mechanisms to
increase lifetime exclusion—were considered preliminary and appropriate for a “further
study” section rather than a full recommendation.

• Some commissioners expressed interest in addressing Hawaii's static estate tax
threshold relative to federal inflation adjustments.
e. Solar Tax Credit / AGI Limit Concerns
Commissioner Enos raised administrative and policy concerns regarding the new AGI-based
limit for solar tax credits.
Discussion centered on:
• Administrative difficulty of requiring taxpayers to certify AGI months before filing
• Misalignment with environmental goals by discouraging participation by high-AGI
investors who undertake large renewable projects
• Potential unintended consequences when actual AGI diverges from projected AGI
• Consultant Dr. Moore noted AGI-conditioned environmental credits often perform poorly
compared to direct income-tax-based redistribution.
Commissioner Enos may bring forward a formal recommendation to remove or revise AGI
limitations.
f. Increasing Tax Credit Take-Up and Filing Compliance
Commissioners expressed interest in improving access to:
• EITC, refundable state credits, and federal benefits
• Facilitated filing programs for individuals with low income or Social Security-only
income
• Staff described existing guided filing tools and public outreach, though uptake remains
limited.
• Consultant Dr. Moore suggested targeted outreach can yield significant financial benefit
to low-income households relative to program cost.
g. Voluntary Disclosure Program Enhancements
Commissioner Yasuda had previously raised this as a revenue opportunity.
Chair White described potential improvements under discussion in other states, including
elimination of anonymous entry to speed up registration and compliance. Commissioners may
consider recommending modernization of Hawaii's voluntary disclosure procedures.
h. Conveyance Tax Reforms
Commissioner Ueoka discussed replacing cliff-based conveyance tax brackets with marginal rate
structures, noting distortions in real estate transactions. Chair White indicated this could either fit
under competitiveness or stand alone.
i. 1031 Exchange Clawback Concept
Commissioner Enos may present a proposal relating to clawbacks for deferred gain through 1031
exchanges, for discussion at an upcoming meeting.

6. DEVELOPMENT PROCESS FOR THE TRC FINAL REPORT
Chair White outlined the administrative process for drafting:
• A template report is being developed by Chair White and will incorporate
recommendations approved by the Commission.
• Due to Sunshine Law restrictions, shared editing platforms are not permitted; instead,
members will submit edits individually to TRC Executive Director Dr. Karacaovali, who
will compile redlines and return consolidated drafts to Chair White.
• Commissioners will present their own topic proposals at future meetings, ideally with 1–
2-page memos or brief slide decks.
