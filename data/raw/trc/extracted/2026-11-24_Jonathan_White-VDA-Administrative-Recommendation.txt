Narrative:
Collections
Collection of delinquent tax debt is an important issue for all tax agencies.1 However, due to
the nature of delinquency, delinquent amounts are among the most difficult amounts of
money to collect. The issue should be considered from both sides.
Within the competitiveness recommendations, the Commission suggested the Department
be funded appropriately so that it can ride along with the economy as it grows. The role of
collections is different. Collections represents the amount of money the Department did not
collect.
Delinquent tax accounts are cumulative and it is impossible to collect 100 percent of all
delinquent amounts. Therefore, delinquent amounts will consistently rise without a write-
off mechanism.

Voluntary Disclosure Program
The potential revamp or addition to the Department’s voluntary disclosure offering is based
on a program that was recently approved by the Streamlined Sales Tax Governing Board
(Streamlined).2 Streamlined is a sales tax simplification organization with 24 member states.
The organization was created in 1999 by the National Governor’s Association and the
National Conference of State Legislatures to simplify sales tax collection.3 The Streamlined
voluntary disclosure program represents just a sliver of what Streamlined does overall.
The Streamlined program provides objective qualification criteria, a near-automatic
acceptance based on those criteria, non-anonymity, a two-year lookback period, and the
ability to immediately register with the state to begin collecting tax and paying. The program
is currently set to last one year.4
The Commission recommends considering a similar program, either permanently or on a
short-term basis. The Commission believes this may draw businesses and others into
compliance that may be wary of the Department’s existing voluntary disclosure program and
its minimum 10-year lookback period.5
There is a connection to collections in that the more taxpayers the state can lure into
compliance early on, the fewer there are out there accruing the size of liabilities that will
prevent them from ever paying.
It is unclear to the Commission whether a VDA program as described here would require
amendments to the law or could be done with the Department’s current administrative
authority.
