# TRC update, 2026-11-19: On Nov 18 the Commission said it was ready to vote on single-sales-factor apportionment (est. +$35M/yr) but deferred the vote for two absent members, and Commissioner Jessica Enos put a Section 1031 "clawback" on the table. The Commission has still taken no vote on anything, and nothing here touches a top-rate increase.

This packet has the final Nov 9 minutes (approved Nov 18 by acclamation), the **draft** Nov 18 minutes (not yet approved), and Enos's 1031 memo (dated Nov 24, posted early). The Nov 18 video and the Nov 24 meeting notice are links only, and I did not read them. The Nov 9 content matches the draft minutes already summarized on 11-13, so the new material is mostly Nov 18 and the Enos memo.

## What was discussed

**Single sales factor (SSF): the vote slipped.** On Nov 18, members "generally expressed support" and several "did not recall any significant opposition." Chair White recommended waiting until the absent members could participate. Judith Chock and Stacey Katakura were excused, so only 5 of 7 were present (White, Enos, Fukunaga, Ueoka, Yasuda). The vote is deferred to a future meeting. This is the same $35M estimate Karacaovali gave on Nov 9.

**1031 clawback (Enos).** Modeled on California, Oregon, Massachusetts and Montana. Hawaii would tax the deferred gain attributable to Hawaii property when a 1031 exchange into out-of-state property is later sold in a taxable sale.
- **Hawaii-only gain:** her example has $1.1M of deferred gain taxed by Hawaii, but not the $300K of later Texas appreciation.
- **Annual filing:** she proposes a California-style annual information filing (FTB 3840 model) until the gain is recognized. It would apply even if the taxpayer has no other Hawaii filing duty.
- **Notice:** a letter to the taxpayer whenever a 1031 exchange involves Hawaii property.
- **Step-up at death:** Hawaii would follow the federal step-up if the owner dies.
- **Double tax:** she suggests a credit for taxes paid to other states.
- **Template:** she prefers Montana's rule (ARM 42.2.308) over Oregon's.
- **Revenue:** the memo has no revenue estimate, only hypothetical numbers.
- **Status:** it is a proposal, not a Commission position. Chair White put it on a future agenda.

**Competitiveness and housing (Nov 18).**
- **Enos:** housing affordability and out-migration. She floated first-time-buyer help and mortgage credits.
- **Yasuda:** a state version of the federal Mortgage Credit Certificate.
- **Fukunaga:** accelerated or bonus depreciation targeted at housing, especially rentals. Several members preferred it to general business expensing. He agreed to develop it.
- **Dr. Dylan Moore (UHERO):** demand-side help "may primarily increase housing prices" when supply is constrained, and supply is the more durable fix.
- **Dr. Baybars Karacaovali:** noted that Act 217 (2026) already expanded the Individual Housing Account.
- **Vacancy taxes:** raised, but they are largely a county matter. Some members want them in the narrative.

**Conveyance tax.**
- **Marginal rates (Ueoka):** he proposed a marginal-rate structure. The Commission agreed to put it on a future agenda, and Karacaovali said background and revenue analysis already exists.
- **Controlling interest:** it remains on the agenda, with no new content.

**Solar credit (Enos).** She objected to the new requirement that taxpayers certify AGI before final income is known. White suggested folding it into a broader tax-administration recommendation instead of a standalone one. No decision.

**Other.**
- **Remote-worker hiring incentive (Enos):** no consensus, with constitutional and commerce-clause concerns.
- **WCR/NCTI and VDA:** White says drafts will go to members after the meeting, with feedback before any vote.
- **Nov 9 content already covered on 11-13:** the WCR/NCTI and VDA presentations, Yasuda's installment-plan idea, Fukunaga on AI in administration, the TAT presentation, and the "competitiveness" list (inflation indexing, pension income, accelerated depreciation, EITC to offset regressivity).

## Fairness and regressivity

- **1031 clawback.**
  - **Who it hits:** it would fall on owners with large deferred gains on Hawaii real estate, such as investors with a $400K basis in a $1.5M rental, mostly nonresident or out-of-state-exiting investors. That is progressive-leaning and closes a gap in taxing Hawaii-sourced gain.
  - **Revenue:** none estimated in the documents.
  - **Step-up at death:** her proposal to match the federal step-up would wipe out the deferred gain at death. That is a significant carve-out, and any revenue estimate should account for it.
- **Housing depreciation and homebuyer credits.** The documents give no cost or incidence. Moore's point that demand-side help may mostly raise prices is relevant to any credit-for-buyers design. Rental-housing depreciation would benefit owners and developers first.
- **SSF.** Still only the $35M total, with no distribution by industry or firm.
- **Solar AGI certification.** Enos frames it as an administrative problem. Removing or softening it would weaken the income-targeting of the credit.
