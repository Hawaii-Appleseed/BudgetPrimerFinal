# TRC update, 2026-11-27: On Nov 24 the Commission cast its first formal vote, 6-0 for single sales factor apportionment, and heard a conveyance-tax rewrite from Jeffrey Ueoka that gives owner-occupants relief and has no revenue estimate yet.

**Caveats.**
- The Nov 24 minutes are a **draft**. The Nov 18 minutes are the final version of the draft covered on 11-19, and nothing in them changed.
- I did not watch the Nov 24 video. The posted link is the same Nov 18 link.
- The Nov 30 and Dec 4 notices are links only, and I did not read them.
- The UHERO final report is about 980K characters. I read its opening and searched it for the top-rate, conveyance and regressivity passages. I did not read it in full.
- The Enos 1031 memo was already summarized on 11-19. It was discussed on Nov 24, and only the discussion is new.

## What the Commission did and discussed on Nov 24

Six of seven members were present. Jon Yasuda was excused.

**Single sales factor (SSF): approved.**
- **Motion and vote:** Commissioner Jessica Enos moved to recommend SSF as a standalone recommendation and as part of the competitiveness package. The roll call was Chock, Enos, Fukunaga, Katakura, Ueoka and Chair Jonathan White, all Aye. Yasuda was absent. The motion carried 6-0.
- **Alternatives:** White said double-weighted sales factors and phased transitions can appear in the report as implementation options.
- **Revenue:** the minutes give no figure. The earlier estimate was about +$35M/yr (see the 11-19 summary).
- **Distribution:** nothing on which firms win or lose.

**Conveyance tax, marginal rates (Ueoka).** The minutes say only that members "generally expressed support." No vote was taken. Karacaovali said revenue estimates can be developed to test revenue neutrality. Ueoka's written proposal is the real content (see the next section).

**Conveyance tax, controlling interest.** Deferred again. White said it "may be more controversial than other recommendations," and Yasuda was absent. No action was taken.

**1031 clawback (Enos).**
- **Examples:** members discussed California, Montana and Oregon. The 11-19 summary named Massachusetts as well.
- **Discussion topics:** annual reporting, credits for taxes paid to other states, and administration.
- **Positions:** members were "generally" interested. Fukunaga asked how large the deferred gains are and what administration would cost. White said more work is needed on interstate credits.
- **Status:** no vote. Enos will keep developing the written recommendation.

**Worldwide combined reporting / NCTI (White).**
- **Framing:** the minutes say White "characterized the topic as one appropriate for further study rather than an immediate legislative recommendation," noting the Legislature (H.R. 179, 2026) has already asked DOTAX to study it. His memo says he wants "buy-in" before doing more work. It is unfinished, with placeholders and notes to himself.
- **Options in the memo:** mandatory worldwide combined reporting, which no state has adopted since the 1980s and he calls "a heavy lift, especially politically." Elective worldwide combined reporting, used by about 12 states, including Colorado's 2026 law with a 10-year election. Conformity to the federal tax on foreign subsidiaries' income (NCTI, IRC §951A) with full factor representation, citing New Mexico's 2026 S.B. 151. He notes the elective route would score negative on revenue, because taxpayers would elect in only if it saves them money.
- **Status:** no vote. Members agreed more discussion is needed first.

**VDA and tax administration (White, with Vice-Chair Judith Chock helping draft).** White calls it "the least complete" writeup. The draft has these parts:
- **VDA pilot:** a one-year pilot with a two-year look-back, objective qualification criteria, non-anonymous sign-up and immediate registration. It is possibly limited to GET/use tax and remote sellers. The pilot is modeled on the Streamlined Sales Tax program. The memo contrasts it with DOTAX's current minimum 10-year look-back (TIR 2020-03).
- **Collections:** private collection agencies, standardized and automatic installment plans, AI, and a stronger Offer in Compromise.
- **General support:** funding for DOTAX, taxpayer education, and a simplified GET-only registration.
- **Solar AGI problem:** the memo cites the AGI certification for the renewable energy credit (Act 24, SLH 2026). Taxpayers must certify AGI by Feb 28, about six weeks before returns are due. It is called "good policy but in practice… not feasible." This confirms the Nov 18 suggestion that it be folded into this package.
- **Status:** no vote. The minutes say "additional work was needed."

**Competitiveness and housing (Fukunaga, Katakura).** Fukunaga laid out slow growth, out-migration and the housing shortage, and argued housing supply is itself an economic-development strategy. The discussion covered accelerated depreciation for housing, credit design, and "tax-exporting" packages. The minutes also list "potential future consideration of wealth tax and estate tax issues" with no attribution or detail. Fukunaga and Katakura will develop housing-competitiveness materials. No formal action.

**UHERO.** The final report is dated Nov 21 and was posted Nov 24. No action was taken, and Dr. Dylan Moore said the table of contents will be expanded.

**Report template (White, dated 11-20).**
- **Structure:** the template has a competitiveness series with three placeholder recommendations.
- **Standalone items:** a credits-and-exemptions framework, SSF, pension-exemption compliance, the 1031 clawback, and controlling interest.
- **Further study:** wealth attraction and WCR/NCTI.
- **Themes:** the Commission is "guided by the principle of tax adequacy" and wants to "avoid creating revenue losses."
- **Credits framework:** White's notes ask for a UHERO-style econometric review and a simpler "pre-game" test. He also asks to re-endorse repeal of small GET exemptions.
- **Pension exemption:** it would get a compliance and data fix only.
- **Status:** a template, not a decision.

## Fairness and regressivity

**Conveyance tax, owner-occupants.** Under Ueoka's proposal the seven bands stay, but each rate applies only to the slice of price in that band. His design claim is that no owner-occupant pays more than today below about $32.6M. His table:

| Price | Today | Ueoka | HB 1410 HD2 | SB 3028 HD2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $750,000 | $1,500 | $900 | $1,125 | $1,050 |
| $1,000,001 | $3,000 | $1,400 | $2,000 | $1,800 |
| $1,100,000 | $3,300 | $1,800 | $2,600 | $2,250 |
| $2,500,000 | $12,500 | $8,900 | $12,250 | $16,300 |
| $5,000,000 | $35,000 | $28,400 | $37,000 | $106,300 |
| $8,000,000 | $72,000 | $57,400 | $84,000 | $246,300 |

- **Below $600K:** unchanged at $0.10 per $100.
- **Indexing:** thresholds would be indexed to the Urban Hawaii CPI.
- **Earmarks:** none. §247-7 is left alone, so the land conservation and rental housing revolving fund allocations continue.
- **Who gains:** relief is biggest in percentage terms between $600K and $2M. The dollar savings are larger at higher prices, for example $3,600 at $2.5M and $14,600 at $8M. The proposal is not targeted at lower-priced homes, since nothing changes below $600K.
- **Non-owner-occupants:** the marginal schedule rises to $2.50 per $100 above $10M. The memo says non-owners pay less below about $3.6M and "moderately more" above that.
- **My arithmetic on that claim:** it holds only partly. At $5M a non-owner pays $40,100 versus $42,500 today. At $8M it is $95,100 versus $88,000. At $10M it is $135,100 versus $125,000. The memo's "above $3.6M" claim looks too broad. Higher payments appear only in a sliver just under $4M and above roughly $7M. Please verify before citing.
- **Revenue:** none estimated. The memo says DOTAX must estimate it and "the schedules should be adjusted accordingly." It concedes that collections fall and that the §247-7 caps could shrink fund distributions. The structure is a tax cut for most owner-occupant buyers, financed in part by the highest-priced investor sales. The net effect is unknown.
- **UHERO context:** UHERO puts the conveyance tax at about $1.1 of cost per net dollar raised, rising to 1.2–1.4 at the top 1.00% and 1.25% rates. It notes possible lock-in effects.

**SSF.** Still no distributional or industry breakdown. UHERO's report has chapters on business taxes and joint reforms that I did not review for SSF.

**Combined reporting / NCTI.** These would raise taxes on multinational corporations, so they would be progressive if enacted. Hawaii's elective approach would raise nothing and could lose revenue. The Commission is not recommending adoption.

**VDA pilot.** It brings delinquent remote sellers into compliance with a shorter look-back. It gives amnesty-like relief to non-filers, with no distributional data given.

**Regressivity language in the template (a point to watch).**
- **GET:** the draft says "there is nothing the Commission can recommend to change the fundamental effects of the GET." It recommends against "drastic change to the GET in the name of reducing regressivity" and points to the income tax as the better tool.
- **Act 46 (2024):** the draft credits the 2024 income-tax reform with doing that work.
- **Package:** the template puts competitiveness first, and there is no equity-side package in it yet.
- **UHERO:** its report says the GET is less regressive measured against spending and that a GET rise paired with income-tax cuts costs the bottom tenth about 1.8% of income. It also finds a top rate of 14% loses about 61 cents of each dollar raised once migration is counted.

## Implications for published work

- **Millionaire's tax report.**
  - **Position:** the Commission remains silent on top-rate increases. Its template is built around adequacy and competitiveness, and wealth and estate taxes appear only as a "further study" placeholder.
  - **UHERO:** its numbers are the new fact to engage. It puts the top income-tax rate at about $2.02 of cost per net dollar raised ($1.42 with no out-migration), and says a rise from 13% to 14% would lose about 39 cents of each dollar before migration and about 61 cents with it. It cites Young et al. for millionaires moving at 2.4% a year against 2.9% for all filers, and Rauh and Shyu for about 45% of California's 2012 increase lost to behavior. Our report should address these directly before the Dec meetings, because the Commission's staff consultant is now on record.
  - **Language:** keep the caution from 11-13. Do not describe the TRC as supportive of a top-rate increase.
- **RETITC analysis.** The draft administration package now explicitly calls the Feb 28 AGI certification "not feasible." It is likely to appear as a recommendation to ease it. That would soften income-targeting, which cuts against Appleseed's targeting argument. This is the strongest new item for RETITC work.
- **Budget Primer.** `report2027/content.md` has a "Modernize the Conveyance Tax" item (lines ~361–368, citing the Appleseed blog) saying rates have not moved since 2009. The Ueoka proposal confirms the 2009 date (Act 59). It is a rate-structure and homeowner-relief proposal, not a revenue raiser, which differs from our framing of conveyance tax as a revenue tool. Do not call this a Commission position. Nothing else in the Primer was checked against the new documents. The editor-driven workflow in CLAUDE.md applies to any change.
- **Coalition conveyance work.** Ueoka's earmark-free design drops the funding for supportive housing, the dwelling unit revolving fund and Hawaiian home lands infrastructure that HB 1410 and SB 3028 would have created. That is a trade-off to raise with partners.
