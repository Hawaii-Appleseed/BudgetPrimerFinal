# TRC update, 2026-11-13: With the final report due Dec. 21, the Commission is converging on single-sales-factor apportionment (est. +$35M/yr) as its lead corporate recommendation, while the conveyance-tax controlling-interest provision Appleseed's coalition asked for is still only a "conceptual outline" and a top-rate increase is not on its list

This packet is a six-week backlog: five sets of minutes (Sept 29, Oct 8, Oct 20, Oct 28, and the *draft* Nov 9 minutes), the UHERO draft report (172 pp., delivered Oct 7), Dr. Moore's Sept 29 roadmap slides, Chair White's two written proposals, Dr. Lacoste's TAT slides, and two Oct 28 testimonies. Sept 18 minutes were also posted (final); they match the draft already covered in the 09-24 summary. **No votes were taken at any of these meetings.** Everything below is discussion, not decision.

## What was discussed

**Single sales factor (SSF) is the front-runner.** Chair Jonathan White presented his written case on Oct 28 for replacing Hawaii's equally weighted three-factor formula (property, payroll, sales) with SSF. Commissioners Fukunaga, Chock, Enos, Yasuda and Ueoka voiced conceptual support; "general agreement" was to present SSF as the primary recommendation and list, without endorsing, alternatives (phase-in, double-weighted sales, industry carve-outs). White will bring it to a vote at a later meeting. On Nov 9, TRC Executive Director Dr. Baybars Karacaovali gave an updated estimate that SSF would raise **about $35 million** in additional corporate income tax revenue under current conditions. Dr. Moore suggested pairing SSF with accelerated depreciation. This is the third consecutive packet where SSF advanced (see 09-24 summary); it is now the best-developed item.

**Conveyance tax: controlling-interest provision.** White's Oct 28 paper (about 17 states tax transfers of controlling interests; Hawaii bills failed in 2013, 2014, 2016, 2024 and 2026) frames the gap as a *horizontal equity* problem, not a revenue one, and gives no Hawaii revenue estimate (the only figure is a $5M/yr estimate for a 2018 New York expansion). Commissioners agreed the inequity is "significant" but said more research is needed on feasibility; Ueoka described practical problems with LLC transfers and exemptions, and Fukunaga warned about avoidance in large commercial deals and the need for a careful "change of control" definition. White "will evaluate whether to prepare a fuller recommendation" — i.e., not yet a recommendation. Separately (Oct 20) Ueoka backed replacing the cliff-based conveyance brackets with marginal rates, which White said could sit under competitiveness or stand alone.

**GET.** On Oct 8 White called a grocery GET exemption a personal "red line," while noting he is one of seven votes. UHERO's draft costs the exemption at **$267.4M** (residents $214.1M, visitors $53.3M). Because visitors keep the $53.3M, a resident-funded income-tax offset also pays for the visitors' share. Fukunaga raised capturing more GET from visitors (Waikīkī special district, with Commerce Clause / Privileges and Immunities concerns). Commissioners asked UHERO to model "what-if" scenarios for pyramiding relief (wholesale-rate changes). On Nov 9 the discussion listed EITC expansion "as a means of offsetting regressive impacts," and Karacaovali noted prior Commissions "generally opposed increased reliance on consumption taxes because of concerns regarding regressivity."

**TAT (Lacoste/Moore, Nov 9; slides marked "interim, under review, not for citation").** TAT is $824M, about 1 in 13 state tax dollars. Synthetic-control estimates of the 1999 (6%→7.25%) and 2009–10 (7.25%→9.25%) increases give a median revenue elasticity of **0.83** and **0.91** (1 = no behavioral response). Those increases raised $72.7M and $42.0M a year (2025 dollars) against $51.6M and $80.9M expected with no response [note: the slide's pairing of the figures to years is garbled in extraction; verify against the PDF before citing]. Conclusion: Hawaii is "safely below" the TAT-only revenue peak, but GET dipped after both increases (−0.09% and −0.03% per 1% TAT rise, imprecise), and visitors pay about 18% of state GET, so the net-revenue answer is less certain. Fukunaga asked about GET interactions; Knox urged "caution"; Moore flagged uncertainty about wage and income-tax effects on residents.

**Income tax / top rate.** The Commission has not taken up a millionaire's tax. On Oct 20 the competitiveness list included "adjustments to income tax rates, especially at high-income brackets" with no direction stated. UHERO's draft estimates raising the top rate from 13% to 14% in 2027 yields about **$46.0M** static, **$41.8M** after behavioral response (elasticity 0.3, which UHERO calls the *lowest* response it will consider, so $41.8M is an upper bound). Returns of $1M+ (1,824 returns) paid about a fifth of the $2.80B total in TY2022.

**Other items.** Governor meeting (Oct 8, Fukunaga and White): interest in attracting high-net-worth people and donation-linked "pre-paid" estate-tax mechanisms. Commissioners treated it as "further study" only. Also floated: inflation-indexing brackets and the standard deduction (Moore: non-indexing is "a stealth tax increase"), a 30-day remote-worker withholding safe harbor, removing the 200-transaction out-of-state seller threshold, VDA modernization (non-anonymous entry, shorter look-back; Yasuda: standardized installment plans), 1031-exchange clawback (Enos, possible proposal), AGI limit on the solar credit (Enos, possible recommendation to remove or revise), take-up outreach for EITC/refundable credits, and worldwide combined reporting / NCTI (White; "for further study," with Karacaovali warning of administrative burden). White told UHERO to keep wealth-tax material brief (Fukunaga disagreed); Knox asked for political-feasibility commentary and Moore declined. UHERO credits total about $600M, only about $150M "economic development" credits.

## Fairness and regressivity

- **Who SSF helps or hurts.** The documents give only the $35M total, no distribution. SSF generally raises tax on firms with Hawaii sales but little Hawaii property or payroll (e.g., mainland and online sellers), which is a progressive-leaning outcome for residents, though Commissioners themselves flagged uneven industry effects (lodging, real estate). Not stated in the documents: incidence by industry.
- **Tax-cut-for-high-income risk.** Competitiveness is the organizing frame (Fukunaga: stagnant real per-capita income; "shifting burdens away from mobile factors (labor, capital)"). Read together with accelerated depreciation, indexing, pension-income taxation and rate adjustments at high brackets, this package could skew toward relief for high earners and capital. Which way "rate adjustments" cut is unspecified.
- **Grocery exemption.** UHERO notes high-income households capture the largest dollar savings but low-income households the largest share of income. Hence the EITC-offset language.
- **UHERO's regressivity framing** echoes COST: ITEP puts the poorest fifth at 14.1%, middle fifth 14.2%, top 1% at 10.1% of income, but UHERO adds that sales taxes look less regressive against spending and that revenue can fund offsetting credits.
- **Capital gains.** The Coalition asked the Commission to consider taxing gains at ordinary rates (currently capped at 7.25% against a 13% top rate from TY2027). No Commissioner is recorded responding.

## Implications for published work

- **Millionaire's tax report.** Two live issues. (1) The Coalition's Oct 28 testimony cites Massachusetts's 4% surtax above $1M (~$3B in FY2025; net out-migration of $200K+ earners fell in year one) as proof high earners do not leave. UHERO's draft is more cautious: top-rate responses reduce yield, and millionaires move at only about 2.4% a year. Our report should cite UHERO's $46.0M/$41.8M per point alongside any revenue figure, since a Commission-commissioned number will be quoted back at us. (2) The Commission has not endorsed any top-rate increase. Don't describe the TRC as supportive.
- **RETITC analysis.** Little new. Oct 8 reviewed credits (about $600M, renewable credits being phased out and noted as concentrated spending), and Enos plans to challenge the new AGI limit on the solar credit. That cuts against Appleseed's targeting argument. Moore said AGI-conditioned environmental credits "perform poorly" compared with direct income-tax redistribution, which is consistent with our critique. Still watching for the revised bottom-line return (below $0.71) in the final UHERO report.
- **Budget Primer.** The TAT card should be updated: UHERO's slide shows the state TAT split by destination, including $824M total and the 2026 "green fee" (0.75 pt) on the 9.25% base, plus Knox's 2.48% to tourism figure from the 09-24 packet. Also, the Primer's SSF/corporate-tax section, if any, should note a $35M estimate, and the "where the burden falls" text should note UHERO's visitor GET share (about 18%).
