# Council on Revenues change, 2026-09-03 meeting — summary

**The Council raised its General Fund growth forecast for FY2027 and FY2028 sharply, revised every other out-year through FY2032, and added a new FY2033 estimate, cumulatively adding about $2.26 billion in projected General Fund tax revenue over FY2027–FY2032 versus the May 21, 2026 forecast.**

## 1. What the Council did

New forecast (September 3, 2026 letter) vs. prior forecast (May 21, 2026 letter), growth rate by fiscal year:

- FY2027: **from 1.0% to 2.5%**
- FY2028: **from 1.9% to 3.0%**
- FY2029: **from 2.5% to 3.0%**
- FY2030: **from 1.8% to 1.7%** (the one downward revision)
- FY2031: **from 3.1% to 3.7%**
- FY2032: **from 3.4% to 3.3%** (also revised down slightly)
- FY2033: **new** — set at 3.7% (no prior comparison; not previously part of the forecast horizon)

Every year in the prior forecast window (FY2027–FY2032) was revised — none were left unchanged. FY2026, the closing base year in the May forecast (-2.5% growth, $9,724,807 thousand), has dropped off the table entirely now that the fiscal year is closed.

## 2. Reasons the Council gave (condensed, their language)

- "Slow tourism recovery, inflation effects, and historical growth trends."
- Current projections are still "below earlier trends" given economic uncertainty from "policy changes at the Federal level and the current war in the Middle East."
- "The multi-year implementation of the individual income tax cuts was also a significant factor considered for the projection period" — i.e., Act 24, SLH 2026 (new income tax brackets/rates, RETITC changes) is now baked into the base case, alongside carryover effects of Act 46, SLH 2024 (standard deduction/bracket increases) and Act 96, SLH 2025 (TAT increase, though the cruise-ship portion is enjoined by the Ninth Circuit and DOTAX is not enforcing it).
- New law changes specifically adopted into this forecast: Act 24 (bracket cuts/new top bracket, RETITC cap), Act 185 (Film Credit cap raised to $60M/yr, sunset extended to 2038), and Act 205 (LIHTC transferability).

## 3. Dollar implications for the General Fund

The letter gives full dollar levels for both forecasts, so the year-by-year dollar impact can be stated directly (amounts in thousands, new forecast minus prior forecast):

| FY | Prior (May) | New (Sept) | Difference |
|----|---|---|---|
| 2027 | $9,822,055 | $10,037,937 | **+$215.9M** |
| 2028 | $10,008,674 | $10,339,075 | **+$330.4M** |
| 2029 | $10,258,891 | $10,649,247 | **+$390.4M** |
| 2030 | $10,443,551 | $10,830,284 | **+$386.7M** |
| 2031 | $10,767,301 | $11,231,005 | **+$463.7M** |
| 2032 | $11,133,389 | $11,601,628 | **+$468.2M** |
| 2033 | — | $12,030,888 | new year, no comparison |

Cumulative increase over FY2027–FY2032: **≈$2.26 billion** more in projected General Fund tax revenue than the May forecast implied. This is a broad-based upward revision to the revenue base the state is planning against, even though two individual years (FY2030, FY2032) saw their *growth rates* nudged down slightly — the dollar levels are higher across the board because the upward revisions compound from FY2027 on.

## 4. Relevance to the Budget Primer's published figures

- The Primer's six-year plan (FY2027–FY2032) should be checked against every growth rate in the table above — all six years changed, not just the out-years. FY2031 (3.1%→3.7%) and FY2032 (3.4%→3.3%) are the two most consequential for any long-range trend line, since they sit at the far end of the six-year window.
- FY2033 is new. If the Primer's plan or any accompanying chart is bounded at FY2032, this is the first official Council figure for a year beyond that window and should be flagged for whoever maintains the plan's time horizon.
- The dollar table above can be dropped in directly to update any general fund revenue table keyed to Council forecasts — no estimation was needed since the Council's own letter provides both the old and new dollar levels for FY2027–FY2032.
