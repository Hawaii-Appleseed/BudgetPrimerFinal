# Council on Revenues change, 2026-10-29 meeting — summary

**The Council raised its calendar-year 2027 Total Personal Income (TPI) growth forecast from 2.0% to 4.5% and held CY2026 at 2.0%; this is a TPI forecast only, and the Council did not revise its General Fund tax revenue forecast.**

## 1. What the Council did

Total Personal Income forecast, October 29, 2026 letter vs. July 30, 2026 letter (calendar years, not fiscal years):

- CY2026: **unchanged at 2.0%** (level moved from $112,102M to $112,484M).
- CY2027: **from 2.0% to 4.5%** (level from $114,344M to $117,546M).

No other forecast years exist in a TPI letter. No fiscal-year General Fund growth rates were touched.

BEA also revised the historical series (the Council's table, new vs. July):

- CY2023: 6.5% to 6.3%
- CY2024: 5.9% to 6.4%
- CY2025: 7.0% to 7.1% ($109,904M to $110,278M)
- CY2021 and CY2022 are unchanged at 7.1% and 2.7%.

## 2. Reasons the Council gave (condensed)

- BEA clarified that the CY2025 TPI figures "assumed settlement payments being made to households in the fourth quarter of 2025, related to the 2023 Maui wildfires," recorded on an accrual basis given "the timing of the final settlement agreement." That produced a "higher than anticipated" 2025 growth rate.
- In July the Council had assumed the settlement money would reach the economy "over several years." BEA has now said it "does not anticipate additional impacts from these settlement payments being recorded in TPI figures for 2026 or later years."
- The Council therefore weighed the elevated 2025 base and "the likelihood of slower measured growth in 2026 due to the absence of a similar transfer payment event." The letter gives no separate explanation for the CY2027 increase. It appears to follow from the settlement money no longer being spread into 2027 and from the higher 2025 base. That is my reading; the letter does not say it.

## 3. General Fund implications in dollars

None can be stated. The letter contains only personal income levels and growth rates. It has no General Fund tax revenue forecast, no fiscal-year growth rates, and no dollar comparison to the prior GF forecast. Turning a TPI change into GF dollars would require an elasticity assumption the documents do not provide, so I have not estimated one. The GF forecast in force remains the September 3, 2026 forecast (see `2026-09-14-cor-summary.md`); the Council's next GF forecast will show whether it reflects this TPI revision. The DOTAX presentation and the October 29 minutes were not extracted in this packet, so I cannot say whether they discuss GF revenue.

## 4. Relevance to the Budget Primer

- **No change to the six-year plan's out-year growth rates** (FY2027–FY2032) is warranted from this packet. Those rates come from the General Fund forecast, which was not revised.
- TPI is a key input to the Council's GF forecast, so the 4.5% CY2027 figure (versus 2.0% previously) is an upside signal for FY2028 revenue in particular. Treat it as a watch item, not a Primer update. If the Primer cites the Council's TPI numbers anywhere, update them to 2.0% (CY2026) and 4.5% (CY2027).
